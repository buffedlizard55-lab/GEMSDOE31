## Why the reported H33 result is the high claim—and what it does not prove

The pinned [byte-level forensic record](../evidence/h33_forensics_20261025.json) identifies H33-2-B2 as a sparse 37,654-positive-pixel raster, with values finite in `[0,1]`. In that comparison it is an exact subset of its D2.8 parent after deleting near-catalogue flank pixels; it adds no pixels relative to the stated parent. Under the competition metric described in the supplied materials (asymmetric Tversky weighting with α=0.2, β=0.8 and a 300 m triangular distance kernel), removing predictions that add little new truth coverage can reduce false-positive mass while preserving overlapping kernel coverage. This gives a **plausible metric-geometric explanation** for why a sparser member of the lineage could rank higher than its denser parent.

That is not a causal finding about private organizer truth. The local geometry does not tell us whether removed dots were false positives, and the claimed 0.2778 has no exact-byte organizer receipt in this repository. The audit artifacts also report different H27 parent counts in different places (40,199 in the [H33 byte-forensics JSON](../evidence/h33_forensics_20261025.json) versus 42,846 in the [later H31-AA session review](2026-10-27_h31aa_session_review.md)); treat the precise parent-count lineage as an **irregularity requiring reconciliation**, not as established fact. The supported conclusion is limited: pruning changes the submitted dot set; it does not prove newly discovered geology or explain the hidden score.

The supplied leaderboard anchors 0.3195 and 0.4413 conflict and are not supported here by an organizer receipt. Accordingly, **we cannot claim or estimate that a new candidate will exceed 0.2778, 0.3195, or 0.4413**. No number in this report is a new candidate's projected score.
