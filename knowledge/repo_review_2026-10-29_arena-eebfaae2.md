### Why H33-2-B2 is the top *reported* focus-site file

The best available causal explanation is an **inference**, not a proven score attribution. The historical H33 forensics record reports that H33 removed 2,545 catalogue-adjacent dots from the H27 40,199-dot field and added no dots. In the reported ladder, the smaller catalogue-pruned fields are associated with higher scores (0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778). Under the official distance-weighted Tversky metric, reducing false-positive contribution while retaining nearby true-fault coverage can improve the index; conversely, dropping a dot that supports a newly mapped fault can hurt recall. The observed pattern is compatible with precision improvement but does not establish causality, because candidate geometry and score provenance are uncontrolled and scores are user-reported.

The official competition page describes DTI with α=0.2, β=0.8 and a 300 m triangular distance kernel, and describes the target as newly identified faults not included in the existing USGS fault database. The rules state that displayed public scores may differ from private evaluation. Therefore 0.2778 is neither a verified official result nor evidence that a new approach will beat it. A score above 0.2778 is possible in principle but **not demonstrated or forecastable from the available evidence**.
