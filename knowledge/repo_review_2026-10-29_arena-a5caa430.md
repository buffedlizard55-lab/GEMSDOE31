## Why the H33-2-B2 claim is high within the supplied focus list

### What is directly measured

The `COMPUTED` artifact forensics in [`docs/data/h33-forensics.json`](../docs/data/h33-forensics.json) and [`evidence/prior_lineage_20261025.json`](../evidence/prior_lineage_20261025.json) show the following pixel-set relationship (all quoted scores remain **USER-REPORTED**):

- H19-5 D2.8: 44,090 dots; the GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 variants share the same dot field in the registered lineage audit.
- H27-4: 40,199 dots; the D2.8 field pruned at a 1-pixel public-catalogue distance.
- H33-2-B2: 37,654 dots; H27-4 with **2,545** additional dots removed at the 2-pixel public-catalogue distance; zero dots on the catalogue in the recorded raster audit, zero added relative to H27-4.

That is a geometric lineage, not an independent geological discovery. It makes **catalogue-flank pruning** a plausible explanation: if competition truth excludes faults already in the public catalogue, predictions concentrated near known faults can add false-positive mass without adding new-truth credit. The competition metric’s recorded official definition uses alpha `0.2`, beta `0.8`, and a triangular distance kernel to `300 m`, so redundant prediction mass can be costly. The shared file geometry does not reveal the organizer’s private truth or which removed cells were true positives.

### What is inference, not proof

Pruning can raise a Tversky score only if the false-positive credit removed is worth more than the true-positive coverage lost. The existing forensics derive the conditional algebraic threshold `lost_T < 0.2*S/(1-0.2*S) × removed_F`. Substituting the **USER-REPORTED** `S=0.2778` produces a **COMPUTED conditional illustration** of roughly `0.0588`; it is not an observed hit rate, a holdout result, or a forecast. The score itself is unverified and private-truth terms are unavailable.

Thus, the most defensible explanation is: **H33 may have scored well because it retained a kernel-spaced field while deleting some catalogue-adjacent predictions that were expensive false positives.** The byte lineage supports the deletion and the metric makes the mechanism plausible; neither proves causation or says that the 2,545 deleted dots were false positives. The reported ladder is confounded by any other candidate-generation changes and by the absence of exact organizer score receipts.

**Can a new method exceed 0.2778? Unknown.** The data in this checkout cannot establish that it can or cannot. It needs a holdout-validated new candidate, a comparable incumbent under the same evaluator/folds, fresh confirmation, complete duplicate checks, and ultimately an exact organizer receipt. The conflicting `0.3195`/`0.4413` claims are not validated targets.
