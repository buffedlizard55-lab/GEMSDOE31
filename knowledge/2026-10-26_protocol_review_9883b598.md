## Why H33 may have performed well

[Existing geometric forensics](../evidence/h33_forensics_20261025.json) identify H33 as D2.8 dots with a two-pixel catalogue exclusion. Sparse emission reduces redundant false-positive cost under a finite-radius kernel; excluding known-catalogue flanks may improve discovery specificity. These are plausible explanations, **not identified causes of the private score**. No private truth is available. The old forensic JSON's phrases about proved live true-positive changes are model-dependent inversions, not measurements; they must not be used as causal evidence.

For fixed truth count N, pooled DTI is `S = T / ((1-beta)T + alpha F + beta N + epsilon)`. Its exact finite-change improvement criterion is
`[1-(1-beta)S] delta_T > alpha S delta_F` (positive denominators). This supports cost-aware coverage, not arbitrary densification. [The algebra is independently regression-tested](../scripts/check_marginal_dti.py). Thinning a copied raster, changing encoding or appending a few dots does not establish an independent method.

Can a genuinely different detector exceed the reported H33 result? **Possibly, but not established here.** The prior H31-X receipt reports HOLDOUT-DTI 0.166133, 95% CI [0.149858, 0.182107], evaluator `gems57-pooled-hide-v2`, 60,988 withheld positives. It is cited, not rerun. Its control win is not a verified win over the best retrained incumbent: fixed prior dot files already pruned against the full catalogue are handicapped in hide-and-recover tests. No leaderboard forecast follows from that result.
