Mechanistic explanation · hypothesis, not attribution

Why a sparse prune could help

The GEMSDOE28 site describes H27-4 as a one-pixel catalogue-flank prune of the D2.8 sparse emission. It reports 44,090 positive cells in D2.8 and 40,199 in H27-4: 3,891 fewer, about 8.8%. This is a post-processing choice, not a newly trained geologic model.

Under the official distance-weighted Tversky metric, a prediction close to a true fault receives more credit than one farther away, while false-positive mass is still penalized. Pruning redundant or low-value off-trace cells can therefore help if enough weighted fault coverage remains. It can hurt if the removed cells cover real unmapped corrections. The hidden fault geometry—not a raster count—determines which case applies.

For baseline DTI D, a small addition is beneficial only when its weighted-credit-to-false-positive ratio exceeds 0.2D / (1 − 0.2D). At the user-reported operating points, this is approximately 0.0573 for 0.2708 and 0.0683 for 0.3195. These are metric-derived thresholds, not measured ratios, official scores, or evidence that H27-4 achieved either value.
