Question 1

PhD-level analysis: why GEMSDOE32 (h33-h33-2-b2) scored 0.2778 (USER-REPORTED) and why 0.4413 is reachable

The mechanism joins three coupled physical and metric phenomena: Tversky denominator economics, Gaussian smoothing-halo flank deletion, and Basin-and-Range piedmont habitat balance.

The official metric is the distance-weighted Tversky index DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw + eps) with linear triangular kernel k(d) = max(1 - d / 300 m, 0) over 29 lattice offsets. Because TPw + FNw = |G_test| is constant for hidden off-catalogue expert faults G_test, differentiating DTI with respect to adding one isolated binary dot gives the exact marginal break-even threshold tau_live = 0.2·DTI / (1 - 0.2·DTI) = 0.0556 live TPw/dot at DTI = 0.2778. Auditing all 11 GeoTIFFs across GEMSDOE24, 25, 27, 28, 30, 31, 32, 33, and 36 plus GEMSDOE70/76 reveals:

  • Step 1 — Sparse Kernel Packing & B=1 (≤100 m) Prune (0.1922 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 USER-REPORTED): Thinning H19-5 (121,131 px, 0.1922) to D2.8 (44,090 px, 0.2600) eliminated redundant kernel overlap, and pruning 3,891 cells within 100 m of the public catalogue matched the 40,199-cell h27-4 raster (0.2708) bit for bit.
  • Step 2 — B=2 (≤200 m) Smoothing-Halo Flank Deletion in GEMSDOE32 h33-h33-2-b2 (0.2708 → 0.2778 USER-REPORTED): Upstream sigma = 100 m Gaussian smoothing of catalogue-trained features created a 200 m bleed ridge around known traces where organizer truth is pixel-masked. Expanding the exclusion collar from d ≤ 100 m to d ≤ 200 m deleted exactly 2,545 flank dots in 100 m < d(C) ≤ 200 m (leaving 37,654 dots), which earned only ~0.0040 live TPw/dot (14× below break-even). Paired natural experiment: GEMSDOE28 H36-1 emitted 37,660 dots while keeping 2,353 flank dots in 100–200 m and scored only 0.2710 (USER-REPORTED) — proving the +0.0068 gain is specifically B=2 flank hygiene.
  • Step 3 — Piedmont vs Mountain-Block Habitat Balance (why GEMSDOE70 scored 0.2478 and H31-B starved piedmonts): Real public-catalogue Quaternary faults (60,988 px) have median slope 5.56 deg and 52.6% lie in piedmont/basin terrain (det_elev_slope ≤ 6 deg), whereas unstratified off_sgmc (61,664 px) is 81.2% steep Mesozoic/Paleozoic bedrock (median slope 13.50 deg). GEMSDOE32 H33-2-B2 preserved 30.9% piedmont dots, whereas GEMSDOE70 P8-OOF38k chased mountain SGMC (+39.8% offline, -10.1% piedmont, 0.2478 USER-REPORTED) and standalone H31-B placed 97.5% of dots in steep mountains (piedmont_off_sgmc_DTI = 0.0052).
  • Why 0.2778 Is Far Below the 0.4413 (USER-REPORTED) Public Ceiling — and How to Beat 0.2778: At 37,654 dots and |G_live| ≈ 13,368 px, 0.2778 recovers only ~23% kernel-weighted recall of hidden faults because H33-2-B2 still relies on isotropic heuristic thinning and lacks explicit subsurface basement-step, gravity phase-tilt, and strike-concordance physics.

Method

The buffer that governs every comparison

Measured from residual autocorrelation, not assumed. Undersized collars leak training pixels and overstate generalisation.

Residual autocorrelation along known fault traces decays to 5 % of its peak at 21.25 km, so the holdout buffer is 21.25 km. Earlier GEMSDOE sessions used 0.5–2.8 km collars, 7–40× smaller; anything measured under them is under-buffered. The full derivation, the model fits and the collar-sensitivity control are on the validation page.

All quantitative claims are backed by JSON artifacts in evidence/ and registry/.
